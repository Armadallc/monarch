Section 8 — Client rights and acknowledgments

Please read each statement carefully and initial in the space provided to confirm your understanding.

InitialsRightStatement
Right to revoke I may revoke this authorization at any time by submitting a written request to Monarch Competency. Revocation will not affect information already released in reliance on this authorization prior to receipt of my written revocation.
Voluntary authorization Signing this authorization is voluntary. Monarch Competency will not condition my treatment on whether I sign this authorization, except where disclosure is necessary for treatment, payment, or healthcare operations, or where treatment is provided solely to create records for a third party.
Potential for re-disclosure Once my information is disclosed, it may be subject to re-disclosure by the recipient and may no longer be protected by federal privacy regulations. However, SUD records protected under 42 CFR Part 2 may not be re-disclosed without my specific written consent, except as otherwise permitted by law.
Right to a copy I have the right to receive a copy of this signed authorization upon request.
Right to inspect records I have the right to inspect and obtain a copy of my health information maintained by Monarch Competency, subject to applicable law.
Court communication & competency restoration Monarch Competency is required by Colorado law and my court order to communicate with the court regarding my competency restoration progress, including admission status, treatment engagement, progress reports, significant incidents, and discharge recommendations. This obligation exists independently of, and is not affected by, this authorization or any revocation of it.

Notice regarding substance use disorder records — 42 CFR Part 2
This information has been disclosed from records protected by federal confidentiality rules (42 CFR Part 2). The federal rules prohibit you from making any further disclosure of this information unless expressly permitted by the written consent of the person to whom it pertains, or as otherwise permitted by 42 CFR Part 2. A general authorization for the release of medical or other information is NOT sufficient for this purpose (see §2.31). The federal rules restrict any use of this information to criminally investigate or prosecute any alcohol or drug abuse patient, except as provided at §§2.12(c)(5) and 2.65.
